## The joint finite question

Retain the separately normalized endpoint and signed-response weighting with lambda1. For discovery, use the existing equal-history/active-padding endpoint measure w and equal-pair/active-padding unchanged-learned measure v. Endpoint energy is the corresponding native H0-to-H1 increment energy E; response energy is the native signed-response energy T. Retain explicit absolute-error handling when a reference energy is zero. \[

\alpha_i\|C_i(a)-Y_i\|^2+
\alpha_j\|C_j(b)-Y_j\|^2+

For an unpaired learned row, enumerate its nine endpoint choices. The paired response must use the actual finite states and the separately formed native Y_j-Y_i, as in executed104 finite evaluation; do not substitute the relaxed q=P(Y-S) expression.

Each row belongs to at most one declared pair. The objective therefore separates into disjoint components:

\[
L_{\mathrm{free},9}^{*}
=\sum_{(i,j)}\min_{a,b}\ell_{ij}(a,b)
+\sum_{i\ \mathrm{unpaired}}\min_a\alpha_i\|C_i(a)-Y_i\|^2.

At implementation, freeze the actual binary64 norm, term-addition and outer-reduction algorithms, candidate enumeration order and canonical smallest-address tie rule before execution. \[
L_{\mathrm{free},9}^{*}\le L_{\mathrm{shared\ route},9}.
\]
